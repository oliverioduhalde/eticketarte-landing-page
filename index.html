Sensitive
Machines

A transaction becomes an artwork. An algorithm, a co-author.

ETicketArte is an autonomous thermal printing device —a reprogrammed box-office ticket printer— that materializes, in a matter of seconds, generative works co-created between artificial intelligence and human authors.

Each time a viewer presses its single button, the system randomly selects one of the prompts, algorithms or rule sets loaded in its memory and sends them to a generative model or program. The result —a text, an image, a score, an instruction for action— is printed immediately on thermal paper: a unique, unrepeatable piece, destined to disappear.

System mechanics

How it works

The core of the device is a single-board computer running custom software. The whole system operates autonomously, equipped with a single physical button. When pressed, the program randomly selects one of the algorithms loaded in its memory and sends the instruction to a generative model —via API— or runs a locally encapsulated algorithm, without going online.

The limit as power

Far from chasing the high definition of contemporary screens, the system leans on the extreme limitation of its hardware: monochrome resolution of 203 DPI and only two colours. That restriction is a poetic strength: it forces every author —human or artificial— into absolute visual and conceptual synthesis. The technical limit becomes a curatorial device in itself.

The ephemeral and thermal ink

Thermal paper is doomed to degrade under light and heat. That condition is not a defect: it is its conceptual core. The viewer receives an instant of human-algorithmic lucidity that will fade, which invites treasuring the moment in memory rather than in the object. Producing to lose, printing to forget, and in that forgetting, remembering better.

The device in operation: the piece comes out already printed.

Open authorship architecture

The conceptual core of ETicketArte is not a closed catalogue of authors, but an architecture completed by those who inhabit it. There is a founding line, but it works as a seed, not as a limit.

Every artist, researcher, teacher or institution taking part can design their own prompt, their own set of generative rules or their own dataset, and load it into the system under the same technical conditions: a button, a random selection, a thermal printer. The device does not hierarchize authorship: all participants are represented by the same output format —the 203 DPI ticket— which levels different disciplinary origins under one shared material and temporal condition.

The final work is, literally, the sum of those who chose to intervene in the system.

Open disciplines

Poetry and literature. Visual arts and drawing. Music and sound composition. Architecture and speculative design. Bioart and science. Performance and action. Dance and choreography. Film and expanded photography. Conceptual gastronomy. Philosophy and critical thought. Graphic design and typography. Communication and new media.

An indicative, non-exhaustive menu of the profiles that can take the system as a support. Each discipline brings its own question about the algorithm, not a closed answer.

Archive of outputs

Every press of the button produces a different piece. These are some of the outputs: naturalist records, generative patterns, gestural strokes, instructions for action. All of them share the same material condition —203 DPI, two values, thermal paper— and the same fate.

Contextual versatility

ETicketArte does not need a gallery: it needs a box office, a counter, or any point where an everyday transaction —buying a ticket, a book, a coffee— can be interrupted by an instant of art. The system is deliberately portable, low-cost and modular: any institution with a point of sale or access can host it, feed it with its own authors, and turn its flow of visitors into an audience for generative art without meaning to.
